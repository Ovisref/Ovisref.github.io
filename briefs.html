Doctoral Research · Technical Brief

Design, Implementation, and Analysis of a Multi-Source Renewable Microgrid with Hybrid Energy Storage for Enhanced Resilience Three renewable sources, one 24 V DC bus, 99% availability.

A field-deployed off-grid microgrid built to answer a question the literature mostly answers by simulation: does resource diversification actually keep critical load energized? Every figure below came off instrumented hardware running a real load — not a model.

01 / Reliability · Source diversification

Diurnal resource complementarity governs availability more than installed capacity.

Run in isolation, the wind turbine was the weakest asset on site — 17.79% availability in a low-wind window, well below rated output. Operated in parallel with solar on the same bus, it cut daily outage duration by 84%. The mechanism is temporal, not capacity-driven: it generated through the nocturnal window, when demand persisted and the solar resource did not.

72-hour sequential test · constant design-basis load · identical conditions
ConfigurationAvailabilityUnserved energyOutage h/day
PV only89.89%10.11%13.52
Wind only17.79%82.21%12.67
PV + wind hybrid99.07%0.93%2.17

During a 15-hour solar scarcity event, nocturnal wind generation peaking at 110 W reduced the cumulative energy deficit by over 76% and held the battery bank clear of its depth-of-discharge limit. I formalised that effect as a Battery Relief Factor — a dimensionless index for deficit reduction under hybrid operation against a single-source baseline.

Scope: results obtained under a constant non-flexible design-basis load. The methodology and the resource-timing conclusion transfer; absolute figures are site- and window-specific, and are presented as such.

02 / Contingency · N-1 validation

Redundancy tested by disconnecting live equipment, not by drawing it.

Two independent PV nodes, deliberately built on different MPPT controllers, tied to a common bus with independent battery banks. I opened a node mid-afternoon under load and ran a five-day single-node contingency to establish whether the redundancy on the one-line was real.

Staged outage scenarios
ScenarioConditionResult
NormalBoth nodes in serviceStable bus voltage, minimal end-of-day droop
Short outageNode-1 PV opened, 45 minLoad rode 67 W → 1,589 W, no abrupt voltage drop
ProlongedNode-2 PV and battery out, ~5 daysSurviving node sustained critical load, 5 diurnal cycles

A roughly 24-fold load increase absorbed during a source failure, with no transient the load could see. The five-day case exposed the real weakness — slow morning ramp under low irradiance, a generation-shape problem rather than a capacity one. That finding is what motivated adding a second resource type rather than simply oversizing storage.

03 / Instrumentation · Data acquisition

Every result on this page rests on continuously logged site data.

The testbed carries a co-located weather monitoring station alongside electrical logging on every source, so generation can be correlated against the resource that produced it rather than against a modelled or interpolated weather product.

Meteorological channels
Solar irradiance (pyranometer), wind speed (anemometer), wind direction (vane), air temperature, relative humidity, barometric pressure, rainfall
Weather logger
CR3000 Micrologger, timestamped CSV records
Electrical logging
MPPT controllers recording array and battery voltage, current, input and output power, charge state and alarm state at 5-minute resolution
Supervisory control
Arduino-based PLC managing power flow, source curtailment and logging of critical operating variables
Custom sensing
Shunt and voltage-divider network on the wind–solar hybrid controller, validated in Multisim before build
Thermal chain
MAX31855 with type-K probes hot side, DS18B20 cold side, ESP32 acquisition to InfluxDB with Node-RED

Over 2.3 million timestamped records have been logged across the meteorological and electrical streams to date. Aligning both to a common 5-minute timebase is what makes the downstream work possible — the forecasting model and the asset-health classifier run on this data rather than on external feeds.

04 / Forecasting · Dispatch

A five-hour forecast built from data the controllers were already logging.

MPPT charge controllers record array voltage, input power, heatsink temperature and internal charge state every five minutes, and almost nobody uses it. I trained an encoder–decoder LSTM on that operational data rather than an external weather feed — no new hardware, no new vendor dependency.

Embedded in a dispatch simulation, the model cut unmet load by roughly 46 kWh, reduced time near minimum state of charge from 79.3% to 75.4%, and raised battery throughput about 13% without increasing cycling stress. Error stays flat across the full five-hour horizon, where the recursive model degrades with lead time — and horizon stability is the property scheduling actually needs.

The finding worth keeping: the model initially showed no advantage at all. 45.2% of the dataset was nighttime zeros, and an error metric averaged over a population that is nearly half structural zeros hides every real difference. Conditioning evaluation on daylight windows made the separation appear immediately — a lesson about choosing the evaluation population, and one that applies to any KPI averaged across periods when the asset isn't working.

05 / Asset health · Condition monitoring

Battery condition monitoring at 99.26% accuracy, with no added instrumentation.

Impedance spectroscopy needs the bank offline. Coulomb counting drifts. Neither survives contact with a distributed installation. I trained a decision tree on 429,027 logged records drawn from two sites in different climates — UNT Discovery Park and an agricultural installation at New Mexico State — to predict charge state from telemetry the controller already produces.

Test accuracy
99.26% · macro-average F1 0.99
Undervoltage class recall
1.00, at 0.06% of the dataset
Dominant feature
Battery voltage — 56% of importance
Class imbalance handled
237 to 134,318 samples · SMOTE on training split only

Feature importance landed where battery physics says it should — voltage first, charge phase second, temperature negligible. That is the check that tells you the model learned the system rather than an artifact of the logging.

Why a single tree over a random forest. Ensemble methods were available and are reported as competitive or better. I used CART because the output is an explicit rule set an engineer can read, audit and disagree with. Where a wrong call means a stranded load or a damaged asset, an explainable model that gets trusted beats a marginally more accurate one that doesn't.

06 / Parabolic Dish CSP–TEG

Parabolic Dish Concentrated Solar Power and Thermoelectric Generator Module in Array

Parabolic dish concentrators reach focal temperatures above 500 °C, and thermoelectric modules convert that heat with no moving parts and no working fluid — at 2–5% efficiency, and only while a stable gradient holds. Building and commissioning this string completed the tri-source system.

Optical
Parabolic dish, high-reflectivity facets ρ > 0.9, cavity receiver with high-conductivity absorber
Conversion
Multi-module Bi₂Te₃ array, series–parallel, graphite thermal interface material
Cold side
Active water-cooling loop, target below 60 °C
Power conditioning
Boost DC–DC converter onto the 24 V bus
